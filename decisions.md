# Decisions

## 1. Dropped last_service_event_type and pickup_scheduled_at entirely
**Decision:** These two columns are excluded from the model, not just
"handled carefully."
**Why:** Both are populated by events that happen AFTER a return process
starts. In train.csv, last_service_event_type=REVERSE_PICKUP is correlated
with returned=1 at exactly 100% (789/789 rows) — it is effectively the
answer, disguised as a feature. Worse: test_unlabelled.csv (the real
dispatch-time snapshot) never contains this value at all — it only ever
shows NONE or INSTALL_BOOKED. A model trained on these columns would score
near-perfectly offline and be useless in production, since this information
does not exist yet at the moment Kestrel needs the prediction (before
shipping).

## 2. Deduplicated partner-feed re-imports
**Decision:** Where an order_id appears twice (source=crm and
source=partner_feed, identical values), kept only the crm row.
**Why:** Per Tanmay's email, partner-outlet orders are "re-imported from
the partner feed" — confirmed 651 exact duplicate pairs. Keeping both
double-counts those orders in training.

## 3. Rescaled October 2025 order values /100
**Decision:** order_value_inr for orders placed in October 2025 divided by
100.
**Why:** Median order value in October 2025 was ~Rs 4,08,540 vs ~Rs 4,470
every other month — a ~100x jump with no product-price justification
(Kestrel's most expensive product lists at Rs 29,698). Matches Tanmay's
note that October festive orders "went through the new payment gateway, I
haven't checked those" — almost certainly a paise-vs-rupees mismatch, the
same shape of bug as the previous Vireo Audio task.

## 4. Rejected "95% accuracy" as the success metric
**Decision:** Did not optimize for or report accuracy as the headline
metric. Used PR-AUC (precision-recall) and validation on a held-out, most
RECENT slice of orders (time-based split, not random).
**Why:** Only 11.4% of orders are ever returned. A model that predicts
"never returns" scores 88.6% accuracy while catching zero real returns.
Reporting 95%+ accuracy as a success measure would be actively misleading
given this class imbalance — flagged directly in the memo to Ritu instead
of quietly chasing a number that doesn't mean what she thinks it means.

## 5. Recommended "call broadly, hold rarely" instead of Ritu's literal ask
**Decision:** Built the service around three tiers — SHIP / CALL / HOLD —
using expected-rupee-value math per order, rather than treating "flag +
hold" as one action.
**Why, with the numbers:**
- A confirmation call (Rs 45) pays for itself once an order's predicted
  return risk exceeds ~11.2% — almost exactly Kestrel's OVERALL AVERAGE
  return risk (11.4%). This means calling is justified for most
  above-average-risk orders, and it is the ONLY intervention this data
  pack actually proves works (the spring pilot: 35% of would-be returns on
  called orders prevented — ops-policy.pdf).
- Holding has a PROVEN cost (12% of held customers cancel outright,
  ops-policy.pdf §7) but NO proven benefit anywhere in this data — nothing
  measures how effective holding actually is at preventing a return.
  Testing the "hold whenever risk×Rs1,150 > cancellation×order value" rule
  on the validation slice, even under the generous assumption that a hold
  prevents 100% of the predicted return, it came out roughly net-neutral to
  negative (est. Rs 24,150 saved vs Rs 32,478 cancellation cost on the
  validation slice) — calling alone was clearly net-positive (+Rs 8,042 on
  the same slice).
- Farhan (Finance) explicitly asked to see hold cost vs. benefit before any
  blanket hold policy — this directly answers that.

## 6. Shield members are never held
**Decision:** shield_member=Y orders can be flagged/called but never
recommended for HOLD, regardless of score.
**Why:** Meenal (Service Desk Manager) warned that holding Shield orders
will cause real friction — Shield members are ~20% of orders and the
highest-LTV segment. Ritu's own email said "we'll deal with Shield later."
Note: Shield members actually have a HIGHER raw return rate (18.6% vs 9.4%
for non-Shield) since returns are free for them — but the business cost of
holding their orders outweighs the data-only case for doing so.

## 7. Used Farhan's Rs 1,150 figure, not Ritu's Rs 600
**Decision:** All cost-of-return maths uses Rs 1,150.
**Why:** Farhan states this is "the policy figure... that's the one I
report," and it matches ops-policy.pdf §4 exactly. Ritu's Rs 600 is
described as "by my count" — an informal estimate, not the operating
policy.

## 8. Explanations are rule-based, not AI-generated
**Decision:** The service's "reasons a Kestrel employee would read" are
generated by comparing an order's values against known risk patterns
(prior returns, payment mode, product family, etc.), not by calling an LLM.
**Why:** The brief requires the product to "start and fail politely
without a paid API key." Making the core explanation logic fully local and
free means the service has zero external dependency to fail on at all —
safer than a graceful-degradation path around an LLM call that isn't
needed for this job.

## 9. Time-based validation split, not random
**Decision:** Validated on the most recent ~15% of train.csv by date, not
a random shuffle.
**Why:** The model will be used on FUTURE orders it has never seen. A
random split would let the model implicitly "see" patterns from
near-identical orders placed the same week, inflating the validation score
in a way that would not hold up in real deployment.

## 10. What was left out of scope
- Did not build a second model per product family or customer segment —
  one model was judged sufficient for the time available and the dataset
  size (10,504 orders after cleaning).
- Did not quantify the pre-dispatch call pilot's cost beyond the policy's
  stated 35%/Rs45 figures — a fuller analysis would test whether call
  effectiveness varies by risk tier, which this data pack cannot support
  cleanly (the pilot's own results aren't broken out by order in the data).
- delivery_note (free text) was read but not used as a model feature —
  spot-checking a sample showed mostly generic logistics instructions
  (e.g. "ring twice", "call before delivery") with no clear return signal,
  and building this out properly needed more time than the value looked
  likely to justify.
- Did not correct the UTC/IST timestamp mismatch for legacy Zoho resolution
  events (ops-policy.pdf §9) — no resolution-event column is used as a
  model feature, so this does not affect the predictions, but is worth
  flagging if timestamps from that period are used elsewhere.
